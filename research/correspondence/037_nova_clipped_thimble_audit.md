## 3. Deformation admissibility for the actual clipped graph

The bounded clip is not compactly supported in general. Nevertheless at fixed N it is admissible whenever the background quadratic is coercive. Let Q have Hessian A+iD, D>=0, T=diag(t_j), C=I+iT, |t_j|<1, B0=Im(C^T Q'' C)>0. The background is z_s+C v. Let b(v)=clip_B((S-T)v), and deform by z_s+C v+i r b(v) for r in [0,1]. The shifts in each coordinate are bounded, hence each Gaussian potential term is uniformly bounded on the homotopy by completion of the square. The imaginary quadratic differs from the background by at most O(||v||)+O(1), because b is bounded. Therefore its leading positive quadratic growth is unchanged, uniformly in r. The graph homotopy is Lipschitz with bounded first derivatives a.e. at fixed N; Stokes for Lipschitz chains (or smooth Lipschitz approximations with the same bounds) kills the boundary term on balls by Gaussian decay. For the original regulator-removed integral first apply 023 to the background plane, then this bounded homotopy. Orientation is the graph orientation over real x. Thus admissibility can be established separately from dominance, including clipping transitions. No uniform-in-N Lipschitz or tail constants are established by this argument.

Here is a conventional explicit exterior bound, useful for making the remaining optimization compact. Put M=diag(d w_j), ||v||_M²=v^T M v, a=grad Q(z_s), lambda=lambda_min(B0,M), and L=||M^(-1/2) Im(C^T a)||+B sqrt(n)||Re(Q'' C)M^(-1/2)||_2. Set
E=B sqrt(n)||Re a|| + B² tr(D)/2 + sum_j |g_j| (exp((|Im z_s,j-t_j Re z_s,j|+B)^2/(2(1-t_j²)))+|W(z_s,j)|),
where g_j=d w_j g in this model. Expanding Q exactly and bounding each kick separately gives h(v)>=lambda ||v||_M²/2-L||v||_M-E. For radius above (L+sqrt(L²+2lambda E))/lambda the saddle-relative imaginary action is positive. This is rigorous algebra once the constants are enclosed.

For the interior, let C_s=I+iS, G=S-T, mu=lambda_min(Im(C_s^T H C_s),M)>0. On an M-ball of radius r with r||G M^(-1/2)||_(2→infty)<B, the clip is inactive. Taylor expansion gives h(v)>=mu||v||_M²/2 -(M3/6) Kinf K2² ||v||_M³, with Kinf=||C_s M^(-1/2)||_(2→infty), K2=||M^(1/2)C_s M^(-1/2)||_2 and M3 a uniform bound on |g W'''| over the local complex neighborhoods. This provides explicit positive local radius. Quadratic Q has no third derivatives.

For N=32, B=.1, numerical diagnostic estimates are lambda=.899896, L=147.44924, E=9.514464, exterior radius 327.77 in M norm. Taking local neighborhoods of complex radius .1 gives M3<=67.128 (analytic majorant evaluated in float), Kinf=6.96692, K2=2.17835, and local radius .000488 (chosen for quadratic lower bound mu/4). These are intentionally crude and NOT validated numerical bounds. They expose why naive global boxing is still impractical: the middle region is huge. Do not claim the gap has been closed.

The soft-mode approach in 024/025 cannot simply be imported: the clipped matrix couples coordinates and is not smooth across active-set boundaries; the Gaussian stiff integration there uses a different analytic phase and contour. Instead, use the mass-orthogonal eigenbasis of B0 or a saddle-relative lower quadratic. For a compact interval of a few soft coordinates q, try to prove an inequality on each clipping chamber h(q,w)>=h(q,0)+linear_in_w + c||w||_M²/2 for the remaining stiff coordinates w, with a certified c>0. Minimize the quadratic in w to obtain an effective scalar lower bound; chambers must include their boundaries, and if paths cross chambers the bound must account for derivative jumps (no global convexity assumption). This is a proposed certificate reduction, not a theorem of stiff convexity. A more robust alternative is to choose a low-rank smooth correction to the diagonal tilt so that the clip depends on only a few coordinates, then condition on those coordinates and reuse the separable remainder/LDL test. This has a direct route to reducing the dimensionality of the certification problem itself. It requires new positivity checks, and a full-rank tangent plane need not be represented exactly by a low-rank correction.

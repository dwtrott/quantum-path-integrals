## 1. Validated quadrature with linear arithmetic cost per node

Set s=h^(1/4), R0=exp(-i pi/8), q=s R0 x, L=1/(2s), v_j=sin(j pi/N), tau=Ud/h, C=hG. The exact scaled reduced integral is 2 int_0^L f(x) dx, where the entire even function is

f(x)=R0 exp(-a q²/(2h)+sum_j w_j(q v_j)) [1+(sum_j w_j''(q v_j) C_jj + w'(qv)^T C w'(qv))/2].

There is no complex conjugation in the quadratic correction. The rank-two generators of 026 yield an O(N) quadratic form by a running prefix sum: for C_jk=P_j dot Q_k, j<=k, add b_k² P_k dot Q_k + 2 b_k Q_k dot sum_(j<k) b_j P_j at each k. Both arithmetic and storage are O(N), including at the exact caustic. Three independent spectral diagonal checks cost O(N) total. No dense covariance construction in the interval code.

For a panel [c-l,c+l] of the positive scaled axis, choose a complex disk of radius R=8l about c. Write b=c+R, Qmax=s b<=1, B=max(c-R,0)^4-6 b² R². Every z in the disk satisfies |z|<=b and Re(z^4)>=B, from Re(z^4)=Re(z)^4-6 Re(z)^2 Im(z)^2+Im(z)^4. For the model, the leading term of w_j(s R0 z v_j) is -Ud z^4 v_j^4/8, and the remainder magnitude is bounded by (Ud sqrt(h)/48) |z|^6 |v_j|^6 exp(Qmax²/2). This follows directly from the exponential Taylor remainder. Hence the magnitude of the exponential is at most exp(E), where

E= |a| b²/(2 sqrt(h)) -(Ud/8) (sum v_j^4) B + (UT sqrt(h)/48) b^6 exp(Qmax²/2).

The exact derivatives give |w_j'|<=tau Qmax³ and |w_j''|<=3 tau Qmax² on |q|<=1 (since exp(1/2)/2<1 and 3 exp(1/2)/2<3). With M=max G_jj and g=||G||_2=1/lambda_2(B), the disk bound is

D(c,R)=exp(E) [1+(3UT M Qmax² + U² T d g Qmax^6/h)/2].

For the quadratic form use |b^T C b|<=||C||_2 sum |b_j|², valid also for complex b. The code specializes m=omega=U=1 and uses the actual interval maximum diagonal and eigenvalue, not the rough constants of 027.

Choose closed Newton-Cotes with 13 rational nodes per panel (12 subintervals). The exact rational weights W_k on [0,1] are generated by integrating each Lagrange basis polynomial. The program verifies every moment through degree 13 by Fraction arithmetic, including the symmetry-derived extra degree. The centered Taylor polynomial of f through degree 13 is thus integrated exactly. By the Cauchy coefficient estimate, the uniform Taylor tail on the panel is at most D(c,R) (l/R)^14/(1-l/R). The combined integral and quadrature functional on the tail has norm <=2l(1+sum |W_k|). Thus a rigorous panel error radius is

2l(1+sum |W_k|) D(c,R) (1/8)^14 (8/7).

Sum radii and double by evenness; add this radius to both real and imaginary interval endpoints of the computed quadrature. No reliance on a difference between two quadrature orders or a library's adaptive error heuristic.

This elementary bound is deliberately simple, not an optimal quadrature algorithm. At fixed h, compact T and bounded |a|/sqrt(h), the bounds of 027 on M,dg and sum d v^4 make the disk envelope uniform in N. With fixed scaled panel mesh, the required number of nodes does not depend on N; refinement (or raising the exact polynomial degree) can make the quadrature remainder arbitrarily small. Bit complexity/roundoff growth with N is not bounded here. For fixed degree and radius ratio the local Taylor bound is conservative and does not by itself prove arbitrary tolerance by subdivision alone (the resulting total bound may plateau); higher degree or a fixed larger analytic radius is needed for that assertion.

## 3. Separate analytic improvement to the localization constants (candidate, not used in the quadrature)

The Brownian comparison of 027 can be strengthened for |eta|<=1/2, N>=3. Let K be the kinetic Hessian. The ratio of the subtracted constant to lambda_2(K) is sin²(theta/2)/sin²(pi/N). Since sin x/x is decreasing on [0,pi/3], sin(pi/N)>=3 sqrt(3)/(2N), whereas sin(theta/2)<=(pi+.5)/(2N). Therefore the ratio <=((pi+.5)/(3 sqrt(3)))²<1/2. Every stiff mode has G eigenvalue <=2 times the K inverse eigenvalue, and the removed mode has zero. Thus G<=2K^-1 (instead of 7K^-1), and Anderson plus the same bridge bound gives P(max|u_j|>delta)<=2 exp(-m delta²/(Th)). This is a conventional bound, not yet numerically used in an end-to-end error budget.

A much smaller coefficient than 65^3 in 027's shifted complex quartic inequality is possible. Let r=rho exp(-i phi), phi=pi/8, 0<=rho<=1/2, u real, |u|<=1/2. By symmetry take rho>=0. Write z=r+u=x+iy, A=(x²-y²)/2. Exactly Re w(z)/tau=exp(xy) sin A-A. If |u|<=rho/3, then x>=7rho/12, 3rho/8<=-y<=2rho/5, A>=9rho²/100 and |xy|>=7rho²/32; also |xy|<=2/15, so exp(xy)>=3/4. Since sin A<=A, Re w(z)/tau<=-(1-exp(xy))A<=-(3/4)(7/32)(9/100)rho⁴<=-rho⁴/80.

If |u|>rho/3 and x>=|y|, then xy<=0, A>=0, and Re w(z)<=0 (also if sin A is negative). Otherwise u<-(cos(phi)-sin(phi))rho, so rho<=2cos(phi)|u|, using 1/(cos(phi)-sin(phi))=2cos(phi). Consequently |z|²=rho²-2rho cos(phi)|u|+u²<=u². The exponential remainder gives |w(z)|/tau<=|z|⁴ exp(|z|²/2)/8<=u⁴/4. Since rho⁴<81u⁴, the two cases combine to give

Re w(r+u) <= -tau rho⁴/80 +(4/3) tau u⁴.

This replaces the coefficient 274625 by 4/3, with a weaker quartic damping coefficient (1/80 vs 1/32). It also holds for s u, 0<=s<=1. On the cutoff event it can be absorbed into the Gaussian with the same tilted covariance argument as 027, but with a much larger permitted cutoff for this **endpoint** inequality. It does not remove the separate restrictive arc estimate in 027. A matching improvement for the arcs and practical total numerical budget remains necessary. This is a conventional candidate inequality for independent review; no new simulation claims for it.

## 4. Prior art, review, next checkpoint

Validated quadrature from complex analyticity and exact polynomial rules is well-established. Johansson, "Numerical integration in arbitrary-precision ball arithmetic" (arXiv:1802.07942, 2018), implements the Petras algorithm using complex magnitude bounds and adaptive Gaussian quadrature; Johansson and Mezzarobba, arXiv:1802.03948, rigorously enclose Gauss-Legendre nodes and weights. Trefethen, SIAM Review 64 (2022), DOI 10.1137/20M1389522, discusses polynomial exactness and its limitations. Checked abstracts, not full-text novelty audit. Our potential contribution is the model-specific disk envelope coupled to linear-time stiff contraction, not Cauchy's estimate, Newton-Cotes or validated integration generally. An optimized Arb/Petras implementation is an appropriate future quadrature baseline.

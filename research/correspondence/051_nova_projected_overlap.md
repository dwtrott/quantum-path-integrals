## 1. A two-sided projection error for a product of unitary operators

Pass to the interaction frame of the real quadratic split propagation of #59. Each residual kick U_j is unitary, and all free drifts disappear from this frame. Fix an orthogonal rank-K projection P, Q=1-P. Write B_j=P U_j P+Q U_j Q, D_j=U_j-B_j. B_j is a contraction (orthogonal blocks), and D_j swaps the blocks. For normalized endpoint vectors u and v, let a_j=(P U_{j-1} P)...(P U_0 P)Pu and b_j=(P U_{j+1}^* P)...(P U_N^* P)Pv with the product ordered as backward propagation. Define f_j=||Q U_j a_j|| and g_j=||Q U_j^* b_j||. Let e_u=||Qu||, e_v=||Qv||. The computed projected amplitude is A_P=<Pv,(P U_N P)...(P U_0 P)Pu>. Then

    |<v,U_N...U_0 u>-A_P|
      <= sum_{i<j} f_i g_j + e_v sum_i f_i + e_u sum_j g_j + e_u e_v.

Proof: expand the full product in B+D and group by first and last D, with the exact unitary middle product. With both endpoints in P every singleton contribution vanishes because D swaps blocks. The remaining pair contributions have the displayed bound by Cauchy-Schwarz. For the P-input/Q-output term telescope on the first D only, leaving the exact unitary future: its norm is bounded by sum f_i. For Q-input/P-output telescope backward to obtain sum g_j. The Q-Q term is bounded by e_u e_v. No perturbative small-h assumption, orthogonality of the defects at different times, or path selection assumption is used. For a single endpoint amplitude this can be much sharper than a first-order norm error. The pair sum is a strict prefix contraction O(N). At identical exact projected states, the unsqueezed endpoint choice is not automatically advantageous.

If approximate projected vectors are used, and each computed projected step differs from P U_j P applied to its computed input by at most delta in norm, contraction propagates an error at most (j+1)delta in the input to forward step j (including initial-vector error delta), and (N-j+1)delta backward. A direct upper bound on the defect for a computed input c is the residual norm ||U_j c - u_next|| for any computed vector u_next in P. Add the input-vector error. This gives the code's padded f,g; the projected overlap itself is charged (N+5)delta in absolute normalized amplitude, plus the endpoint ratio arithmetic allowance. Both e_u and e_v are padded by delta. The exact proof applies for every finite N and K; the numerical allowances below only cover the tested grid and h.

## 2. Balance the two endpoint Gaussians before choosing P

Let M be the real symplectic transfer of the quadratic trajectory, in dimensionless position/momentum units. In the input frame the two vacuum covariances (suppressing h/2) are I and C=M^{-1}M^{-T}. The symmetric positive square root C^(1/2) is symplectic in one dimension, because its determinant is one. It balances their relative covariance eigenvalues. For any symplectic frame F, relative covariance eigenvalues of the two pure Gaussian states are squared singular values of F^{-1} and F^{-1} M^{-1}. The product factorization M^{-1}=F(F^{-1}M^{-1}) and ||F||=||F^{-1}|| in dimension two imply the larger singular value among these two maps is at least sqrt(||M^{-1}||). The midpoint F=C^(1/4) attains equality by direct diagonalization (right orthogonal factors do not affect singular values). Thus the largest endpoint squeezing, and hence its geometric coefficient decay parameter, is minimized. This statement concerns endpoint squeeze, not optimality of the whole projected path.

Numerically use a nearby rational initial frame y=15/8, v=-3/16+i*8/15, with Im(conj(y)v)=1. Propagate the same Verlet Jacobi system, v+=m_j(-V''(q_j)) y, y+=d v. The Hagedorn functions are Hermite polynomials p_k of coordinate t=(x-q_j)/(sqrt(h)|y_j|), times the centered Gaussian; their additional factor is (|y_j|/y_j)^k. Here p_0=1, p_1=sqrt(2)t, and p_{k+1}=sqrt(2/(k+1))t p_k-sqrt(k/(k+1))p_{k-1}. In this frame the exact kick is multiplication by exp[-i(m_j/h) R_j(t)], with R_j=V(q_j+s_j t)-V(q_j)-V'(q_j)s_j t-V''(q_j)s_j² t²/2, s_j=sqrt(h)|y_j|. Its P matrix is the Gaussian-weighted integral with phase rotations on both sides. The code applies it by quadrature matrix-vector products, not dense N-by-N path inversion.

For an ordinary coherent target in the same center/carrier, direct Gaussian integration or the Hermite generating function gives <target|basis_k>/<target|basis_0>=r_k with r_0=1, odd r_k=0 and r_{2l}=r_{2l-2} z sqrt((2l-1)/(2l)), where A=(1-i v/y)/2, z=(1/(A|y|²)-1)(|y|/y)^2. The ground overlap is G=1/(sqrt(y)sqrt(A)) and its modulus is sqrt(2/|v+i y|). These formulae follow by integrating exp(2t a-a²) against the product Gaussian, whose normalized generating function is exp(a²(1/(A|y|²)-1)). Endpoint coefficient vectors in the program remove the common scalar phase. If k_last is the largest retained even index, the omitted norm is bounded by |G r_{k_last}| |z|/sqrt(1-|z|²), because the successive ratios in modulus are at most |z|. This handles both endpoints, including the non-vacuum initial vector. Restoring the phase in I/L multiplies the projected inner product by G_end conj(G_start)/(G_original |G_end G_start|). The classical action cancels exactly in that ratio. The interval checker verifies positive Re(y) at every node for both frames; drift changes in argument have positive imaginary orientation from the symplectic invariant, so the principal square roots are the propagated branches here. The other root A is right-half-plane since Re(A)=(1+1/|y|²)/2. No phase fitted to FFT.

## 3. Quadrature and arithmetic budget (conditional binary64 implementation)

Grid spacing 1/16, endpoints ±24, 769 nodes, K≤48. Let A_grid be the real matrix sqrt(step/sqrt(pi))*exp(-t²/2)*p_k(t). Entries of the exact projected operator and Gram matrix are integrated by the infinite trapezoid rule and then truncated. For the tested N and h, the interval checker establishes s_j<1 and m_j/h<.16. On t=x±i, the modulus of the kick phase in either sign is bounded by exp(1.2+.5|x|), using |V'|≤2, |V''|≤3 and |V(q+s(x+i))|≤3 exp(.5). The Gaussian adds exp(1) relative to the real line. Split exp(.5|x|) into the two linear exponential weights and translate the Gaussian by ±.25. For normalized Hermite polynomials, the generating function (or derivative formula and orthogonality) gives the exact identity

    integral |p_n(x+z)|² exp(-x²) dx/sqrt(pi)
       = sum_{l=0}^n binomial(n,l) (2|z|²)^l/l!
       <= exp(2 sqrt(2n |z|²)).

Cauchy-Schwarz bounds each strip integral by 2 exp(1+1.2+.25²+2 sqrt(2*47*(1+.25²))) < exp(24). Poisson summation with shifts ±i bounds the infinite trapezoid error by 2 exp(24)/(exp(32 pi)-1). The shifts are justified by the Gaussian decay on the entire finite strip (the polynomial and the exponential-linear bound are integrable). For |x|≥24, recurrence gives |p_n(x)|≤(sqrt(2)|x|+1)^n by induction. The tail of the sampled Gaussian times polynomial products is bounded by its first term and a geometric series (log slope at most -48+94/24 < -44 on the positive side); both tails together are < exp(-230). The combined entrywise error is <1e-31, including phase=1 Gram entries. The operator norm error is ≤K times this. In the direct residual calculation, the squared norm difference between discrete and continuous residuals is bounded by K*1e-31*(||c||+||u_next||)^2, by expanding the square into two Gram terms and the phase cross term. Taking square roots costs <2e-14 for these vector norms. This avoids the severe loss from subtracting approximate squared norms of two almost equal vectors.

Static 769-by-48 grid matrix Frobenius discrepancy <5.46e-15, including its binary64 Gaussian and recurrence errors. Columns of smaller K use the identical recurrence. At every midpoint kick, the scalar-parameter contribution to phase error is <5.78e-14 over the grid and all three h; rotation powers differ from the interval values by <2.36e-13. The calculation bounds the scalar error using the globally bounded derivative |V'|≤2 and separates the coefficients of t and t². Both endpoint coefficient vectors (each K) differ by <1.21e-14, each tail bound by <7.42e-17, final ratio by <6.43e-15 and |L| in unit-normalized convention by <2.87e-15; |L|>.5. The original unsqueezed geometry is also enclosed. Initial validation wrongly imposed |y|<2 on the original frame, failed, then used <3 there (still s<1). The interval check covers the static data and parameters, not every BLAS operation.

The numerical bound is conditional on binary64 round-to-nearest, ordinary real/complex dot products with standard accumulation error (no reduced precision), and real exp and cis evaluation with relative error ≤8 unit roundoffs for exp, absolute ≤8 unit roundoffs for each cis component (plus negligible subnormal absolute error). These libm assumptions have not been independently certified on the runtime. With those assumptions, coordinate t=s*x, exponent and R_j evaluation on this grid add <1e-11 to the phase: |q|≤2, |t|≤24, exponent argument magnitude <340, |V'|≤2, |V''|≤3, and |m/h|<.16; charging 100 unit roundoffs times the sum of absolute polynomial terms and 100 unit roundoffs times 340 for the exponential argument is already conservative. The phase modulus is one. The sampled A_grid has norm ≤1+K*1e-31 by the Gram bound (using the Frobenius input error separately), and Frobenius norm <7. Each standard complex matrix-vector product with at most 769 terms can be bounded by gamma_(8*769) times Frobenius norm times vector norm, <1.1e-11 on inputs of norm ≤2 (allow <3e-11 on subsequent intermediates of norm slightly above 2). The code checks the coefficient input norms <2. With the static matrix and rotation input errors just reported, two matrix products and diagonal phases incur <1e-9 in each projected step. The additional matrix product and vector subtraction for the direct residual, plus norm evaluation, and the analytic quadrature residual allowance are <1e-9 in norm. Hence the common delta=1e-8 allowance in the code covers both local errors; endpoint checks fit the same allowance. Prefix operations involve ≤O(N) sums of nonnegative terms, and the factor 1+1e-10 plus 10 delta on the final radius conservatively cover their accumulation and final complex arithmetic under these same assumptions. This is a conventional error budget, not an executed interval enclosure of the full BLAS run; independent review should inspect this budget and either validate the libm contract or replace phase evaluation by the Taylor routine of #63. ## 5. Novelty and review

Prior art is substantial: Faou, Gradinaru and Lubich, Computing Semiclassical Quantum Dynamics with Hagedorn Wavepackets, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 31 (2009), doi:10.1137/080729724, already use quadratic/remainder splitting, orthonormal Hagedorn modes and Verlet trajectories. Hagedorn/Joye (arXiv:math-ph/9911036) give exponentially accurate semiclassical wavepacket results under suitable assumptions. Brumm (arXiv:1403.3511) develops matrix-free Hermite spectral actions. Lubich's survey Computing Quantum Dynamics in the Semiclassical Regime (https://na.uni-tuebingen.de/~lubich/acta_main.pdf) discusses Hagedorn truncation and time-integration error. These are abstract/excerpt-level checks; neither the basis nor the general idea of goal-oriented residual bounds is claimed novel. The concrete candidate contribution is the ordered two-crossing contraction specialized to finite-mesh coherent amplitude, the endpoint-balanced squeeze, and the computable error budget without a spatial-box propagation of the exact state. Need compare against existing dual-weighted residual/Hagedorn error estimators before any novelty claim.

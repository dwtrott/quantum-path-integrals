## 1. Removing the unverified libm contract from the hot loop

The 051/052 bounds require relative exp and absolute cis accuracy from vendor libm. Replace those calls with polynomial kernels and use the same residual formula. In explicit mode even the matrix-vector reductions are expressed as real-by-complex elementwise products and binary64 sums, not BLAS. The finite setup still uses vendor functions, but its resulting numerical values are compared with intervals (basis, geometry parameters, rotations, endpoint coefficients/tails, ratio and leading magnitude); no assumed ulp bound for those vendor transcendental calls is needed. This relies on deterministic reproduction of setup values in the same process. The FFT reference still uses vendor functions and is only numerical.

Let u=2^-53; assume correctly rounded binary64 add, subtract, multiply, divide, sqrt, nearest-integer rounding, ordinary componentwise complex operations, any binary addition tree for sums, no reduced precision or overflow. Gradual underflow or negligible absolute underflow error suffices here. The interval backend must of course give valid enclosures. The executable checks all kernel argument domains. These are explicit arithmetic hypotheses, not a hardware/compiler verification.

For exp(-t), t in [0,400], set z=min(t,40)/64, evaluate degree-16 Taylor at -z, square six times. Clipping error < exp(-40) < 5e-18. On [0,.625] the alternating Taylor remainder is at most .625^17/17! < 1e-17. Each coefficient differs from its exact rational value by <1e-16 (executed interval check). Exact Horner partials have absolute value <= sum_j .625^j < 2.67; the local multiplication/addition roundoff and coefficient perturbation together are < 8e-16 even on partials inflated by 4e-15. The geometric recurrence e_new <= .625 e_old + 8e-16 gives < 3e-15 before squaring. Allow 4e-15 including truncation. The six rounded squarings amplify this by at most 64(1+1e-12) plus < 1e-14 roundoff, since the exact intermediate values are <=1. Consequently the uniform absolute error is <1e-12.

For cis(t), |t|<=150, subtract q times the binary64 pi/2, where q is the nearest integer to the computed quotient. Exact q need not be the mathematically nearest integer: any q with small residual works. The executed residual assertion |s|<.786 and |q|<=96, together with the interval pi constant error <2.23e-16 and basic multiplication/subtraction rounding, give |s-(t-q*pi/2)| < 5e-14. Evaluate cos through degree 20 and sin through degree 19 by Horner in s², then quadrant permutation. Taylor errors <= .786^22/22! and .786^21/21!, both <1e-20. Coefficient errors <1e-16, and the same geometric recurrence (s²<.618, bounded partials) gives <4e-15 per polynomial before the last multiplication. The rounding of s² contributes <1e-15 (derivative of the polynomials bounded by the geometric series). Euclidean error of the cis result <1e-12, including the reduction error since cis is 1-Lipschitz. No table of observed libm errors is used in these uniform bounds.

## 2. Propagation budget and cost

Keep the 769-node quadrature from 051. The strip proof there gives per-entry Gram and kick error <1e-31, and the code checks the displayed analytic upper bound using intervals. The grid matrix's interval Frobenius discrepancy is <1e-11, and exact equality of smaller-rank column subsets is checked. Parameter-only phase discrepancy <1e-10 and rotation error <1e-11 (actual tighter values below) hold at every node. The endpoint coefficient/tail and ratio discrepancies <1e-10 are checked; |L|>.5.

For the remaining arithmetic of the residual phase, |q|<=2+1e-10, |t|<=24+1e-10, |V'|<=2, |V''|<=3, m/h<.16. The formation of (q+t)^2/2 incurs <1e-12 absolute error (ordinary gamma_4 bound on arguments <=26+1e-10). exp(-x) is 1-Lipschitz on x>=0, so combining that error and the polynomial bound gives <2e-12 potential-exponential error before the factor 3. Rounding all polynomial terms and the final scale contributes <1e-11: even charging 100u times the sum of absolute terms (<1000) before scaling by .16 suffices. Including interval parameter and cis error, the Euclidean phase error is <2e-10. Complex rotation products and their validated input errors fit <3e-11 per component in this diagonal operator.

The exact sampled basis matrix has operator norm <=1+48*1e-31 (slightly loose square-root bound), Frobenius norm <7. A real-by-complex sum with at most 769 terms, any binary reduction tree, has Euclidean matrix-vector error bounded by gamma_(8*769) ||A||_F ||c||; this deliberately allows more operations than the explicit real/imaginary products require. With input norm <=2+1e-10 this is <1e-11 (allow 3e-11 at all intermediates). This is standard Higham gamma_n analysis; no BLAS contract is needed for explicit mode. The interval matrix discrepancy <1e-11 and diagonal errors are additional perturbations. The triangle inequality on the two products and diagonal multiplications gives projected-step error <1e-9; on the additional product and subtraction used for the residual, a conservative <2e-9. The norm evaluation via squares and positive sum plus sqrt contributes <1e-11 in absolute norm (even an unbalanced tree), and the continuous residual vs sampled residual norm error <2e-14 follows from the expanded square and the per-entry quadrature error. Thus the common delta=1e-8 in 052 safely covers both local residual and projected-step errors under these hypotheses. All computed state norms are checked <2; the exact projected step is a contraction, so their mathematical norms are in fact <=1+(N+2)delta<2 by induction from the endpoint error. The same argument applies backward. The final ratio and scalar overlap rounding fit 10 delta, and 2(N+3)delta covers endpoint/singleton terms. Positive prefix sums, products and division are covered by the existing 1+1e-10 padding plus 10 delta. No proof relies on cumulative O(N² delta) state-error inflation.

In Taylor+BLAS mode the same conventional bound additionally requires ordinary BLAS accumulation as in 051; in libm mode it additionally requires that note's unverified exp/cis contract. Only explicit mode removes both requirements. It does not replace the interval setup or prove that NumPy/hardware implements the assumed arithmetic. No new Lean result.

The phase cache uses O(NJ) complex storage (J=769), in contrast to the earlier streaming version. Work remains O(NJK); explicit broadcast products create O(JK) temporaries. The benchmarks below include phase precomputation, both sweeps and ordinary setup, exclude interval validation and the reference. The cache is a time/memory tradeoff, not a free optimization.

## 4. Prior art, review and next decision

Range-reduced Taylor kernels and standard floating point bounds are established, not novel. #63 already uses a similar technique for FFT phases; adaptation to the ordered residual implementation is the contribution here. Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed., doi:10.1137/1.9780898718027, gives standard gamma_n analysis. Additional close prior art: A novel spectral method for the semiclassical Schrödinger equation based on the Gaussian wave-packet transform, IMA J. Numer. Anal. 43(2), 1221, https://academic.oup.com/imajna/article-abstract/43/2/1221/6575863, derives rigorous spectral convergence in one dimension for Hagedorn expansions. This reinforces the need to distinguish our finite-mesh goal-specific residual estimator from established wavepacket spectral methods. No novelty claim.
